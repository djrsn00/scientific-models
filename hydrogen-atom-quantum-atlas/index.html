Equations & interpretation

INSPECT
One spatial wavefunction
ψ n ℓ m = R n ℓ ( r ) Y ℓ m ( θ , φ )
ρ = | ψ | 2 , ∫ ρ d 3 r = 1

R sets the radial structure; Y sets the angular structure. The cloud samples their full probability density. Each dot is a sample of the distribution of one electron.

Quantum numbers & angular momentum

n = 1, 2, … sets the principal energy level. ℓ = 0, …, n − 1 labels orbital angular momentum. m = −ℓ, …, +ℓ labels its z component.

L 2 : ℓ ( ℓ + 1 ) ℏ 2 , L z : m ℏ

This atlas selects m = 0. That means Lz = 0, not zero total orbital angular momentum for p and d states. Electron spin s = ½ is separate from these spatial wavefunctions.

Radial and angular nodes
N radial = n − ℓ − 1 , N angular = ℓ

A node is a zero of the wavefunction. The gold surfaces locate nodes of the selected real m = 0 states; they are guides, not material shells.

Sign, phase & a stationary density
Ψ ( r , t ) = ψ ( r ) e − i E n t / ℏ

The time factor changes a common phase, leaving |Ψ|² unchanged. For these real states, cyan and magenta show opposite signs at one chosen phase reference: 0 and π.

Opposite colors do not mean opposite charge. A common sign reversal changes neither density nor the relative π phase between regions. Display fades do not simulate a physical transition.

Coulomb model & length scale
[ − ℏ 2 2 μ ∇ 2 − e 2 4 π ε 0 r ] ψ = E ψ
a μ = 4 π ε 0 ℏ 2 μ e 2

μ = memp/ (me + mp) is the reduced mass. Here aμ ≈ 0.05295 nm; the conventional Bohr radius a₀ uses me instead of μ.

Coordinates describe electron–proton separation. The central proton marker is enlarged for visibility.

Energy levels & one allowed transition
E n ≈ − 13.6 eV n 2
E i − E f = h ν = h c λ

The photon exhibit uses 2p, m = 0 → 1s, m = 0: an allowed electric-dipole transition with Δℓ = −1 and Δm = 0. Its approximate energy is 10.2 eV and wavelength 121.6 nm.

The energy arrow and photon glyph are schematic. They do not trace an electron moving between clouds.

What this atlas represents

Analytic, nonrelativistic spatial eigenstates of isolated hydrogen. Fine structure, Lamb shifts, hyperfine structure, external fields, and radiative dynamics are omitted.

Point concentration represents probability. Brightness and bloom aid visibility. The analytic state is normalized over all space; the finite sampling window and its captured probability are reported separately.

The main orbital keeps a common physical length scale while the camera changes framing. Comparison thumbnails are individually fitted and display their physical cutoff radii.
