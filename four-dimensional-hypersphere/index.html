Mathematical structure

EXPAND
The 3-sphere in four dimensions
S³ ⊂ ℝ⁴
x2 + y2 + z2 + w2 = R2

S³ is a three-dimensional curved surface embedded in ℝ⁴. It is the boundary of a four-dimensional ball.

The luminous grids and points sample this geometry. Their glow, thickness, and color are visual encodings.

Hyperspherical coordinates
x = R sin χ sin θ cos φ y = R sin χ sin θ sin φ z = R sin χ cos θ w = R cos χ

0 ≤ χ, θ ≤ π
0 ≤ φ < 2π

Three angles locate a point on S³. Varying one angle while holding the others fixed traces a coordinate curve. Not every coordinate curve is a great circle.

Fixing the fourth coordinate
x² + y² + z² = R² − w₀²
ρ= R2 − w0 2

For |w₀| < R, the section is an ordinary sphere surface S². At w₀ = ±R it contracts to a point. For |w₀| > R there is no section.

The gold guide isolates this section without 4D rotation. Its intrinsic radius differs from its apparent radius after projection.

Mapping four dimensions into three

FINITE PERSPECTIVE

p = d(x′, y′, z′) / (d − w′) d > R

The rotated point q′ = (x′, y′, z′, w′) is projected from (0, 0, 0, d) onto the hyperplane w = 0. Keeping d > R avoids a projection singularity on S³.

ORTHOGRAPHIC REFERENCE

p = (x′, y′, z′)

Orthographic projection discards w′. It is also the limit of the perspective expression as d tends to infinity. The resulting 3D scene is then rendered by a separate camera onto the screen.

Rotation in an XW plane
x′ = x cos α − w sin α w′ = x sin α + w cos α y′ = y,   z′ = z
QᵀQ = I   ·   det Q = 1

These rotations preserve x² + y² + z² + w². The six coordinate planes are XY, XZ, XW, YZ, YW, and ZW.

S³ itself is unchanged by rotation. Its marked grid, circles, and reference points reveal motion through the fourth coordinate as their projections change.

Great circles and geodesics
γ(τ) = R(u cos τ + v sin τ) u, v ∈ ℝ⁴ ‖u‖ = ‖v‖ = 1,   u · v = 0

A plane through the origin intersects S³ in a great circle. Its arcs are geodesics of the round 3-sphere.

Projection generally changes lengths and curvature. A projected geodesic need not be straight in the 3D view. The drone follows an illustrative viewing path.

Stereographic projection — reference
p = R(x, y, z) / (R − w)

Projection from the north pole (0, 0, 0, R) maps S³ with that pole removed onto ℝ³. It preserves angles locally, but changes lengths.

The omitted pole corresponds to infinity. This is a mathematical reference; the main guided view uses a finite projection viewpoint outside S³.
